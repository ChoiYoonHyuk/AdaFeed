# Airport activity classification on published route networks

The primary endpoint is the mean of three country/network-specific out-of-fold multinomial log losses (each network receives equal weight) on all USA, Europe and Brazil Airports from the struc2vec authors. Smaller is better. Each outer fold withholds labels on one fifth of nodes, stratified using seed 20261121. The complete unweighted topology is visible transductively. No activity, node identifier or neighbor labels enter the features. The recorded activity quartiles are the targets.

Use FP64 on the H100 to form root return moments on B=A/rho(A) through degree 14. Degree/the graph maximum and spectral radius can be obtained from static adjacency. Let p=log(1+d)/log(1+dmax). Give all arms log(1+d), p and log(1+1000 diag(B^j)) for j=2,...,8. Add six features from (B+p_i I)^j (local), (B+Diag(p))^j (spatial), or B^j (longer static, j=9,...,14). For both shifted arms use j=3,...,8 and the same log1p scaling. A secondary one-hop control gives the same six extra coordinates to both shifted arms: neighbor mean of p, its square and cube, within-neighbor variance, minimum and maximum. Also report degree-only and long-static baselines. All heads are standardized on training nodes and use multinomial logistic regression, C=1 with an intercept. No validation tuning. Verify matrix-power vs local binomial and cubic identity numerically. Degree is available to the parameter policies and readouts in all shifted arms.

Report the primary endpoint, each network's log loss and accuracy, and descriptive fold-level differences without treating dependent folds as independent replications. Before looking at any predictions, define low/high spatial contrast by whether abs(sum_j B_ij^2(p_j-p_i)) exceeds the network median. Report paired differences in mean loss per stratum (spatial minus local), and high-minus-low differences, by network. No population inference across airports or networks. All arms and strata retained regardless of outcome. Record source and script digests.
